RSI
14-day Relative Strength Index, 0–100. Under 30 means the stock has fallen fast relative to its recent moves.
Vol ×
Today’s volume divided by the average of the previous 20 days.
Trigger alerts
Plain notices that a research condition turned true at the latest close, for example the close moving back above its 20-day average, RSI(14) moving back above 30, a new 60-day low, or an earnings date coming up. Each fires once, when the condition changes from false to true. They use daily closes only, the thresholds are untested defaults you can change in config/alerts.toml, and nothing here predicts returns or suggests an action.
5d resid.
The 5-day move left after removing what the stock’s own index would explain, using the stock’s usual sensitivity to it (beta, estimated from the previous year of daily moves and excluding the last 5 days; capped between 0 and 3). A large negative number means the drop is larger than the index move and the stock’s usual sensitivity would explain. Beta comes from one year of one stock’s data and is noisy (about ±0.2–0.3); stocks with a negative estimate are floored at 0, so they get no market adjustment. It describes today’s move only; it has not been backtested and is not a signal.
Earnings tag
Shown when a company reported within the last 5 trading days or reports within the next 10. A drop right after a report is a different situation from a drop with no news. Dates come from a free source, can be missing or off by a day, and are not point-in-time, so they are never used in the backtest.
5d vs index
The stock’s 5-day move minus the move of its own country’s index where one is configured (matched by ticker suffix), otherwise its regional index, otherwise STOXX 600. Index types are mixed: the DAX is a total-return index (it includes dividends) and the Stockholm and Oslo indices are all-share, not large-cap. A big negative number means the drop is not just the market.
